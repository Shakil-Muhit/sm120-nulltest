# GPA — Zhou, Krentel, Mellor-Crummey et al., "GPA: A GPU Performance Advisor Based on Instruction Sampling" (CGO 2021)

**Reading-order item 4.** Tag: ledger THREAT/OVERLAP.

## Research question
Can a profiler estimate, per optimization, the speedup a programmer would get from removing a class of stall, and are those estimates accurate against achieved speedups?

## Main contribution (authors' claims)
Instruction-sampling attribution of stalls to code, and speedup estimators for two forms: stall elimination S^e = T/(T − M) and latency hiding S^h = T/(T − min(A, M^L)), where T is total time and M the attributable stall time. A per-optimization table (Table 3) of estimated versus achieved speedup.

## Evidence and scope
26 optimization instances across the paper's benchmark kernels on a V100; estimated-vs-achieved geomean error 4.1 %, range 1–42 % (loop unrolling 42 %, code reordering 26 % worst); achieved speedups 1.01–3.53×, geomean 1.22× (`scratchpad` extraction of the PDF, `gpa.txt`, Table 3). Execution mode: individual CUDA kernels; not framework-level.

## Relationship to this study
- Overlap: the ledger's Y = T_attr × f and Z = Y / T_wall is the same "time × removable fraction" estimator (`docs/ledger_spec.md`; `LEDGER.md` notation line f = 1 − 1/s).
- Difference: GPA attributes stall cycles inside a kernel from hardware samples; the ledger attributes kernel classes from node-level traces and applies roofline or byte-model fractions. GPA validates every row; the ledger will validate only the rows the Phase-3 session intervenes on (E1, E2, E3, E4a), and its C-grade rows stay unvalidated.
- Scientifically meaningful: the ledger is a reporting discipline (graded, stacked with G1/G2/G3 groups, scored post hoc with a realized/ceiling column and a coverage check), not a new estimator.

## Consequence for this work
The ledger is introduced as "estimator: GPA's S = T/(T − M); validation protocol: GPA's and Daydream's per-optimization estimated-vs-achieved table". The realized/ceiling column and the coverage line ("no realized value may exceed its ceiling") are the ledger's own bookkeeping; any realized value above its ceiling falsifies that row's attribution and is reported as such.

## Unresolved
Whether GPA's error range transfers to framework-level classes; nothing in GPA covers cudagraph replay or host-side costs.

# The contribution test (Phase 3 §7; written last, 2026-09-22)

Question put by the external reviewer: after accounting for the closest prior work, what useful conclusion does this study establish that readers did not already have?

## 1. The central finding, named

**Finding.** For a band-split audio forward (MelBandRoformer, 60 ragged-K bands) on an RTX 5090 under PyTorch 2.8, the choice between the repaired single-graph compile (C3) and pad-and-batch restructuring (H) flips with chunk length, and the trace-attributed cost model that was registered to predict the flip does not transfer: it placed the crossover at 5.9 s, the measurement brackets it between 6 s and 8 s, and at 4 s and 6 s the model under-predicts H's advantage by 0.7–0.8 ms because its per-band GEMM-efficiency term is wrong at mid lengths, while its fixed terms (batching benefit, cast cost) are confirmed by the interventions. Weight representation (pre-cast or frozen without inlining) moves C3 by 0.5–1.4 ms without moving the crossover's direction; compile mode (max-autotune) moves C3 by 4.8 ms at 8 s and 0.2 ms at 100 ms by replacing 178 cuBLAS GEMMs per pass with Triton templates while leaving every parameter cast in place, and is the larger lever at the long operating point.

The four tests:
- (a) **Stated cause.** The cost difference C3 − H is decomposed into a fixed batching benefit (0.55–0.58 ms, from the two traces), padded FLOPs at H's rate, a kernel-efficiency term (per-band C3 GEMM rate versus batched H rate as a function of frames) and small non-GEMM terms (`docs/phase3/padbatch/decomposition_table.json`, `docs/phase3/e9/e9_cost_model.json`). The cause of the failed prediction is located in one term: the efficiency term's rate curves, fitted from two operating points, saturate too fast (`docs/phase3/e9/e9_residual.json`: C3's rate would have to be 53 rather than 71 TFLOPS at 401 frames to absorb the residual alone).
- (b) **Falsifiable in this study.** E9 registered the crossover length and the signed differences at two never-measured chunk lengths before the pod was created (`prereg_amendments.md` v0.2, `docs/phase3/e9/e9_cost_model.json` with its hash in `session3_config.json`); the measurement falsified them (verdict FAIL, `e9_check.json`: +1.13 ms observed vs +0.37 predicted at 176,400; +0.67 vs −0.02 at 264,600). E1 (cast removal) and E5 (structure variants) were the controls for the fixed terms and confirmed them in direction: pre-cast realizes 0.73 / 0.55 ms against a 0.92 / 0.86 ms ceiling (100 ms values from clock-clean repeats only), and the 100 ms wall floor moves with mask-estimator bytes at 3.6–3.7× the fp16-read bandwidth floor, the signature of a cast that reads fp32 and writes fp16 before the GEMM reads it.
- (c) **Consequence for a decision.** Below about 6 s of audio per call, pad-and-batch is the faster deployment on this class of hardware and framework; at 8 s the repaired compile is, and max-autotune on top of it is worth 4.8 ms (16 %); a cost model built from two traced operating points should not be used to place the switch, a measured bracket should. Freezing as shipped in torch 2.8 (nn-module inlining on) is a net loss of 2.5 ms and leaves every cast in place; the same freezing with inlining off is the largest single-config gain at 100 ms (0.67 ms, 21 % of the wall; from clock-clean repeats, since the registered 5 % clock rule flags the first repeat of several 100 ms pairs and the 100 ms headline cells of this session).
- (d) **Closest prior work does not already give the answer.** See §2 and §3: batched-versus-per-problem crossovers are known to exist and to be size-dependent (Abdelfattah et al., ISC 2016), engines select implementations by shape empirically (TensorRT profiles; llama.cpp PR #27637), and MoE work compares grouped and padded GEMMs along M; none of them attributes the crossover to named terms for a ragged-K band split, registers the prediction, or reports that the attributed model fails at held-out lengths and which term fails.

The working candidate named in the handoff (which costs set the crossover, how compile mode and weight representation move it, and whether the attributed model chooses correctly at lengths it was not built on) survives as the central finding, with its answer being that the fixed terms transfer and the efficiency term does not.

## 4. What a reader learns that they did not have before

A deployment engineer choosing between padding a ragged-K band split into one batched GEMM chain and running the repaired single-graph compile of the per-band code now has a measured rule for this workload class on consumer Blackwell: pad-and-batch wins up to at least 6 s of audio and loses at 8 s, and the margin at the short end is 40 % of the wall at 100 ms. They also learn that the natural way to place the switch, a cost model attributed from traces at two operating points, gets the fixed terms right (the batching benefit and the cast cost, both confirmed by intervention) and the length-scaling term wrong, so the switch must be placed by measurement rather than by that model, and the reason is the shape of the per-band GEMM rate curve, not the padding arithmetic. Finally, they learn two things about the compile stack that the documentation does not say: torch 2.8's freezing does nothing about autocast weight casts unless nn-module inlining is off (and costs 2.5 ms when it is on), and max-autotune's 4.8 ms at 8 s comes from GEMM kernel selection with the casts untouched, so the two levers address different costs; whether they add was not measured.

One caveat on the 100 ms numbers: with the 5 Hz clock sampler the registered 5 % drift rule flags the first repeat of several 100 ms pairs and all three compiled 100 ms headline cells of this session (SM clock 2572–2992 MHz across restarts), so the corrected audit is INCOMPLETE for those three cells and every 100 ms pair value above uses clock-clean repeats only; the 8 s numbers are unaffected.

## 5. Verdict (advice to the owner, who decides)

**Workshop or short paper, or an arXiv report with the full evidence bundle, not the ISPASS main track.** The central finding is substantive and falsifiable, and it was falsified in the direction that makes it useful (the model that should have placed the switch could not); but it rests on one model on one GPU, the cost model that fails is the study's own two-point construction rather than a method the community uses, and the confirmed parts (size-dependent crossovers exist; shape-specialized selection is practised; graph breaks and casts cost time) are established elsewhere. A main-track claim would need a second model or GPU and a rate model with enough traced points to be tested rather than merely bracketed; the registered rule forbids adding those experiments to rescue the verdict, so the honest venue is the smaller one, with the audit, the registration and the ledger's scored ceilings as the methodological contribution alongside the finding.

# Research directions from the corrected SM120 MelBandRoFormer evidence (Stage 2, 2026-09-22)

Starting evidence: the corrected Stage-1 report `docs/phase4/EXPERIMENT_REPORT_session4.md` (session 4, one RTX 5090, audit 170/170 in the pinned environment), its statistics `docs/phase4/stats_v2_session4.json`, the trace attribution `docs/phase4/inventory_diff_session4.json`, the Phase-3 record on `main` (labelled by session and GPU), the incidental log `docs/phase4/STAGE1_INCIDENTAL_QUESTIONS.md`, and the Phase-3 related-work notes `docs/related_work/`. Stage-2 pilot probes were pre-registered in `docs/phase4/stage2/PROBES.md` and ran on ml-beast GPU 4 (RTX 5090, GPU-eb6e4a8a…, shared host, pinned venv); their outputs are in `docs/phase4/stage2/results/` and are **engineering evidence**: directional, never paired with pod numbers, never a headline.

Three candidates were developed. One is recommended; one is narrowed to a methodological rule because prior work already explains it; one is retained as promising but expensive. Rejected alternatives and limitations are listed at the end.

## Candidate 1 (recommended) — Producer-side operand warming: when removing a redundant producer kernel slows its consumer

### A. Research question
In latency-bound inference, a cast (or any kernel that materializes an operand immediately before its consumer) leaves that operand resident in L2 when the consumer runs. Removing the producer — by pre-casting weights, by Inductor freezing/constant folding, or by any "eliminate the redundant kernel" optimization — removes its cost and its warming. **Under what conditions (operand bytes relative to L2 capacity and to the per-pass working set; consumer regime, i.e. small-M GEMMs vs compute-bound GEMMs) does the lost warming cost a measurable fraction of the removed producer's time, and can the net effect be predicted from trace quantities (bytes, launch counts, L2 size, per-kernel regime) rather than re-measured per model?**

### B. Motivating evidence
- Session 4 (one GPU, matched): freezing with inlining off gains +0.743 ms [0.740, 0.750] at 100 ms (C3I → C3G), while the traces show 0.857 ms of parameter-cast kernels removed and the GEMM class **0.374 ms slower** — the same ten cuBLAS/CUTLASS kernels with the same launch counts, the dominant 16×16 wmma kernel (154 launches/pass) taking +242 µs (`docs/phase4/inventory_diff_session4.json`; per-kernel comparison from the session-4 `ideal_costs` rows). At 8 s the same kernels are only +0.13 ms slower (+49 µs on the dominant 128×128 kernel) against a +1.296 ms gain. Established, matched, same GPU.
- Session 3 (other GPU, matched): pre-casting the weights **without** freezing (arm C3P) slows the GEMM class by the same amount at 100 ms (**−0.352 ms**, identical kernels — the session-3 dominant kernel: 155 launches, +243 µs; re-analysis with `docs/phase4/probes/inventory_diff_pairs.py` on the session-3 traces; C3 → C3P at 8 s: −0.05 ms) while removing 0.75 ms of the 0.85 ms of cast time (60 of the 420 casts remain) and gaining 0.53–0.60 ms (`SESSION3_RESULT.md`). So the slow-down belongs to "the cast kernel is gone", not to constant folding. Established, descriptive attribution.
- Model facts: 456 MB of fp16 weights per pass (`full_C3_c4410_r1.json: param_bytes_fp16`), RTX 5090 L2 = 96 MB (NVIDIA RTX Blackwell whitepaper; 128 MB on the full GB202), so the weight set streams through L2 every pass and only a freshly written or read weight is resident for its GEMM; the largest per-GEMM weight is 4.7 MB (1536×1536 fp16).
- Pilot B1 (engineering, `docs/phase4/stage2/results/probe_b1_gemm_locality_4410.json`): the 300 `nn.Linear` calls of one 100 ms forward (88 distinct shapes), each timed in isolation from a cold L2 (256 MB flush before every iteration), GEMM-only CUDA-event time, launch-weighted per pass: fresh cast then GEMM **2,650 µs**; pre-cast GEMM alone **3,100 µs** (+450); read-touch of the weight then GEMM **2,422 µs** (−228); an unrelated cast of the same size then GEMM **2,931 µs** (+281). Kernel names identical in all 88 shapes. Reading: warming the operand (by writing or reading it) reproduces and exceeds the in-model excess (+352 / +374 µs); the unrelated-cast control contrast (b − d = 169 µs) is consistent with operand-specific warming for most of the 450 µs, but condition-specific clocks were not recorded, so a generic preceding-kernel component is bounded only by that contrast, not established. Qualifications: event timing quantized at ≈ 2 µs; the shared host idles at low SM clocks between iterations (the unrelated-cast condition controls for wake-up effects); engineering GPU.
- Conjecture (not established): the mechanism is L2 residency of the operand; TLB/page-locality is the live alternative (a read-touch warms both).

### C. Mechanism and competing explanations
1. **L2 residency (candidate).** The cast writes the fp16 weight (write-allocate in L2); the GEMM reads it from L2 instead of HBM. Predicts: effect ∝ operand bytes that fit in L2; largest when the consumer is latency/bandwidth-sensitive (small M); vanishes when the consumer is compute-bound or when the whole weight set fits in L2 (all weights stay resident across passes).
2. **Address-translation locality (alternative).** The cast output is a fresh caching-allocator block (recently touched pages), whereas the parameter lives in a large cold region; TLB misses rather than L2 misses. Predicts: a *sparse* touch (one access per page) warms as well as a full read; a full read does not help beyond that.
3. **Kernel/config selection (alternative).** Different operand provenance → different cuBLAS heuristic. **Ruled out** for this model: kernel names and launch counts are identical in the traces (sessions 3 and 4) and in all 88 pilot shapes.
4. **Clock/power state (alternative).** Any preceding kernel raises SM clocks before the GEMM. The unrelated-cast control contrast (b − d = 169 µs) is consistent with a small generic component; clocks per condition were not recorded, so it is not an established bound.
What distinguishes 1 from 2 now: nothing (the read-touch warms both). What might: a sparse-touch condition or L2-bypass reads — suggestive only, since sparse reads also fill cache lines and do not guarantee the translation state the GEMM will see.

### D. Intended generality
Execution class: inference graphs with a materialized-operand producer immediately before a memory-sensitive consumer — autocast/cast kernels before GEMMs (any fp32-master-weight model under autocast: the "autocast re-cast" pattern of F7), **dequantization kernels before GEMMs** (weight-only-quantized LLM decode with unfused dequant), KV-cache dtype conversions, LoRA merges, layout conversions before attention. Relevant properties: operand bytes ≤ L2, per-pass working set ≫ L2, consumer at small M (batch-1 decode, short audio chunks, streaming), consumer kernel that does not prefetch/stream enough to hide HBM latency. Expected invariants: excess per consumer call ≈ f(bytes, L2-vs-HBM latency/bandwidth), independent of the producer's arithmetic. Conditions under which it should change or disappear (conditional hypotheses, not tested): large M (compute-bound; 8 s here: +0.05–0.13 ms), models whose weights fit in L2, fused producer–consumer kernels (dequant-GEMM fusion removes the question), operands larger than the L2 available to them. Not "all transformers" and not "any producer–consumer pattern": a checkpoint with ≤ 4.7 MB weights per GEMM on a 96 MB L2 is one point of the (bytes, L2, M) space, and the dequant pattern is a transfer hypothesis.

### E. Closest prior work
- **Warm-vs-cold cache benchmarking practice** (RRZE `gpu-benches` README; standardkernel.com, "This Kernel Was Faster Yesterday"; jan.ai, "How we benchmark GPU kernels"; NVIDIA Nsight Compute `--cache-control`) establishes that L2 state changes kernel time and that microbenchmarks should flush — the effect is known **as a measurement artefact** for isolated kernels.
- **L2 persistence control** (CUDA Programming Guide §L2 cache control, compute capability ≥ 8.0, `cudaAccessPolicyWindow`; Lei Mao's demo, ≈ 20 % at a 3 MB persisting window) establishes that residency can be steered deliberately.
- **Framework Tax (EMNLP 2023)** establishes that fixed framework overheads dominate at small inputs (general overhead, not this cast result); **NVIDIA's torch CUDA-graph integration guidance** recommends disabling the autocast cache under capture, i.e. accepting the redundant casts and calling their cost usually negligible; the measured cast cost (0.86–0.93 ms per pass here) and the removal results are this study's own (Phase-3 F7 and E1). Inductor freezing (torch 2.8 `torch._inductor.config.freezing`, constant folding of weight casts) is the standard removal.
- **GPU microarchitecture microbenchmarks** (Jia et al., "Dissecting Volta", arXiv 1804.06826; Turing T4, 1903.07486; Jarmusch et al. Blackwell, arXiv 2507.10789v2 — Phase-3 note 10) give L2 latency/bandwidth figures for the per-call model.
- What prior work establishes: warm/cold L2 changes kernel time; casts cost time; residency is controllable. **What this study establishes:** in a whole compiled model, removing the cast slows the *unchanged* GEMM kernels by 44–47 % of the removed cast time at the 100 ms point (session 4: 0.374 of 0.857 ms; session 3 pre-cast: 0.352 of 0.751 ms) and by 7–14 % at 8 s (0.05 of 0.775; 0.130 of 0.923), reproducibly across two pre-cast implementations (freezing, explicit pre-cast) and two sessions/GPUs; the isolated pilot reproduces the size and shows an operand-specific warming contrast against an unrelated-kernel control. **What remains unanswered:** whether it is L2 or translation locality; a quantitative rule (bytes, L2, M → net); whether it holds for dequant-GEMM patterns; whether a cheap prefetch (touch kernel or `cudaAccessPolicyWindow`) recovers the loss under CUDA graphs. No source found that treats "the removed producer was a prefetch" as a first-order term of an optimization's net effect; the search covered PyTorch docs/issues, NVIDIA guides and the microbenchmark literature and is not exhaustive.

### F. Potential contribution and usefulness
An explanatory characterization and a decision rule: "removing a producer kernel is worth (producer time) − (consumer excess), where the excess is predictable from operand bytes, L2 size and the consumer's regime"; a diagnostic for "gain smaller than removed cost" situations (visible in any two-trace comparison as identical kernels getting slower); potentially a compiler/runtime heuristic (keep the cast fused as a prefetch, or insert a touch/persisting window before latency-bound consumers). Users: torch.compile/Inductor freezing users, quantization-pipeline authors (dequant kernels), TensorRT/serving engineers choosing between fused and unfused producers, benchmarkers attributing per-kernel deltas. It changes the reading of "cast removal" results in the literature: the net gain is smaller than the cast time at small M, for a reason that is not the cast.

### G. Smallest discriminating experiment
Baseline: C3G (frozen, no casts) at 100 ms. Intervention: C3G with a read-touch of the **actual frozen fp16 operand** of each targeted GEMM — the `_frozen_param*` constants held by the compiled graph module, not the fp32 `nn.Linear.weight` that C3G still keeps — issued immediately before that GEMM (a custom op that reads the constant, so it survives the compiler and is captured in the CUDA graph); operand identity is verified by pointer equality with the GEMM's input before any interpretation. Scope: the 60 calls per pass of the dominant 1536×1536 projections (4.7 MB weights), not all 300 Linear calls (300 touches would cost ≈ 0.6 ms of launches alone and swamp the ≈ 0.3–0.45 ms at stake). Control: the same touches over *unrelated* buffers of equal size. Measurements: matched pairs C3G vs C3G+touch and C3G vs C3G+unrelated-touch (registered 5×50, 3 restarts, threshold 0.10 ms) **and node traces; the criterion is the GEMM-class device-time recovery on the touched subset** (session-4 excess on that subset ≈ 0.24 ms), not the wall-time net, which is expected to be about zero or negative because the touches cost launches. Quality checks: validation and SDR gates unchanged. Expected outcomes: **operand warming** → the touched GEMMs recover most of their excess and the unrelated touches do not; **generic preceding-kernel effect** → the unrelated touches recover as much; **not reproducible under graph replay** → neither recovers, with the operand identity verified (then the pilot's cold-L2 isolation does not transfer to steady-state replay and the direction narrows to unfused execution). A sparse-touch arm may be added as a suggestive, not decisive, L2-vs-translation contrast. Pilot B1 already ran the isolated version (results above).

### H. Transfer or boundary test
- **Regime boundary on the same model:** the excess per GEMM must shrink with M: 100 ms → 1 s → 8 s (session 4 already gives +0.37 → +0.13 ms for the class; the 1 s point is new data).
- **Workload transfer:** a weight-only-quantized decoder (int4/int8 → fp16 dequant kernel then GEMM, batch 1) on the same GPU — a different producer (dequant) with the same structure; predicts that fusing dequant into the GEMM or removing an explicit dequant kernel shows the same "unchanged GEMM slower" signature in traces. This is genuinely new evaluation data (never inspected here).
- **Operand-size boundary:** operands larger than the L2 available to them — reachable on the same GPU by a wider variant of the projections (structural variant machinery from Phase 3) rather than by changing GPU (a different card changes bandwidth, compute and clocks at the same time and would not isolate L2 capacity; a 6 MB L2 still holds a 4.7 MB operand). Predicts the per-call excess to stop growing with operand size once the operand no longer fits.

### I. Decision criteria
Supports: G's touch arm recovers ≥ 50 % of the touched subset's GEMM-class excess and the unrelated-touch arm does not. Weakens: recovery < 20 % under graph replay with operand identity verified (isolation does not transfer). Narrows: effect present only for operands above some size or only with cold clocks; a dequant workload without the signature narrows the scope to cast producers. Rejects: unrelated touches recover as much (generic effect). Outcome labels: *not reproduced* (G fails on the pod with the registered protocol); *implementation-specific* (holds for autocast casts only); *already explained by prior work* (if the effect is fully the textbook warm-cache artefact and offers no predictive rule beyond "flush before you time"); *promising but unresolved* (G supports, H incomplete). An unsuccessful prefetch optimization does not eliminate the question: the characterization stands even if no cheap remedy exists.

### J. Effort and dependencies
Pilot: done (minutes). G: 1–2 days engineering for a graph-capturable touch op plus one matched pod session (≈ 1 h at 100 ms; the harness, arms and audit exist and are reused); the sparse-touch arm is a small addition. H: 100 ms/1 s/8 s from the existing arms (no new code); the quantized-decoder transfer needs a second model and dequant path (2–4 days; local GPU only until final numbers); the small-L2 GPU needs a rented A5000-class pod (≈ 1 h). Expertise: CUDA memory hierarchy, Inductor custom ops. Uncertainty: the graph-replay transfer is the main risk; the per-call model's constants need the microbenchmark figures.

## Candidate 2 (narrowed to a rule) — Ranking validity under in-process compile contexts

### A. Research question
When two compiled variants of the same module are built in one process (A/B harnesses, ensembles, factory patterns), which cheap observables certify that each variant's compiled artefact equals its standalone one, so that a ranking measured in that process is trustworthy?

### B. Motivating evidence
Session 4 PB (established, matched, both build orders): the second-built C1-family arm runs 15.2 ms (41 %) slower whichever arm it is; its build exhausts dynamo's recompile limit on the shared `Attention.forward` frame (`[7/8] torch._dynamo hit config.recompile_limit (8)`, mel_band_roformer.py:127 — one code object serving the 60-band and 801-frame attention axes), compiles with 5 instead of 8 graph breaks, and the profiled matched process shows the excess as GPU-busy time (49.3 vs 34.7 ms; 215 more kernels, 51 vs 96 graph launches). Standalone runs never hit that limit. Break-free arms (C3 family) are unaffected (A/A −0.009 ms). The Phase-3 "C1P is 15 ms slower" (E3) was this artefact. Pilot A1 (engineering, `docs/phase4/stage2/PILOT_RESULTS.md`): an A/A C1 process (identical options) shows **no** exhaustion and no gap at the default limit (C1 − C1#2 = −0.14 ms; only the standard einops hit) — the shared budget is exhausted only when the two builds' cache entries invalidate each other (different Inductor options, as in the pod's C1/C1P pairs); the discriminating C1 vs C1P intervention reproduces the pod artefact at the default limit (−14.75 ms, `[7/8]` hit) and **removes it at limit 64 (−0.22 ms, no hit)** — causal confirmation on the engineering host (`docs/phase4/stage2/PILOT_RESULTS.md`).

### C. Mechanism and competing explanations
Mechanism: dynamo's cache and recompile limit are per code object; two instances of the same module share both, so the second build's guard failures (the two attention axes present sizes 60 and 801 to one code object; dtypes) exhaust the budget and the frame falls back to eager, with previously compiled nested entries reused where their guards pass (torch 2.8 `convert_frame.py`). Alternatives: allocator/cudagraph-pool pressure (not supported: the excess is GPU kernel time, not idle or host time), guard-evaluation overhead (not supported: same reason). The intervention (A1) tests the mechanism directly.

### D. Intended generality
`torch.compile` workloads with shape/dtype recompiles where more than one instance of a module class is compiled per process; the artefact scales with the fraction of the graph that falls back; it was not observed for the measured C3 configurations, but break-free graphs are not immune in general (guard specialization can exhaust a shared budget — the isolation DevLog's examples include full graphs); it vanishes with isolated caches or separate processes.

### E. Closest prior work
- PyTorch user guide, "Dealing with Recompilations" (docs main, 2026): the limit (`recompile_limit`, alias `cache_size_limit`, default 8; accumulated limit) and the consequence — "all future compilation attempts will result in the function being skipped (run eagerly)", "all nested function calls WILL be skipped (Dynamo will try to use previously compiled bytecode for the nested functions)".
- PyTorch DevLog, "Dynamo Isolate Recompiles" (2026-05-04): names the problem exactly — "multiple `torch.compile()` calls targeting the same underlying code object share a single cache and recompile limit" — and ships `torch.compile(isolate_recompiles=True)` (post-2.8 versions; not in the pinned 2.8.0).
- PyTorch forum thread "Multiple compiled versions of the same model"; TorchBench (arXiv 2304.14226) methodology; vLLM's torch.compile write-up (cache reuse across a session).
- What prior work establishes: the sharing and the eager fallback, and an upstream fix. **What this study establishes:** a quantified ranking inversion (41 %) in a controlled matched harness, its log/trace signature, and that it hides inside "valid" records unless per-frame counters are read. **What remains:** whether a bounded diagnostic (recompile-limit hits + break/kernel/launch-count equality between standalone and in-context builds) is sufficient in general — a modest question.

### F. Potential contribution
A harness rule and diagnostic (already applied here: PB's standalone/in-context equality tests), a corrected reading of Phase-3 E3, and a caution for A/B benchmarking of compiled models with breaks. Useful to benchmark authors (TorchBench-style suites, MLPerf submitters, serving A/B tests). Not a research program: the mechanism is documented and being fixed upstream.

### G. Smallest discriminating experiment
Pilot A1 (done): the same matched process with `recompile_limit` 8 vs 64. Predicted under the mechanism: no `[7/8]` hit and block medians within ≈ 0.5 ms at 64. Observed: A/A C1 process — no hit and no gap at either limit (identical options reuse entries); C1 vs C1P — −14.75 ms with the hit at 8, −0.22 ms without any hit at 64. Follow-up when a torch build with `isolate_recompiles=True` is pinned: the same pair with isolation.

### H. Transfer or boundary test
A different model with data-dependent control flow (graph breaks) in a two-instance process, and the same model with `torch._dynamo.reset()` between builds; both change the cache-sharing condition and nothing else.

### I. Decision criteria
A1 removed the artefact: the outcome is *already explained by prior work* (documented sharing and eager fallback; upstream `isolate_recompiles`) — keep as a rule: separate processes, or a raised/isolated recompile budget, for arms that compile with graph breaks; publish per-frame counters and standalone/in-context equality checks with every matched record. No research program follows from it.

### J. Effort
Minutes (done as A1/A1b). Rule adoption: harness change only; the Phase-3 E3 record stays as published with its re-labelling.

## Candidate 3 (promising, expensive) — What governs the per-band vs pad-and-batch ranking across chunk lengths for ragged-K band computation

### A. Research question
For band-split spectrogram models whose per-band projections have different K (frequency bins per band) and a shared M (frames × batch), the two execution strategies — per-band GEMMs (C3 family) and pad-and-batch (H family) — swap ranking with chunk length even after strong generic optimization. **Which measurable properties — the band layout's padding waste, the per-band GEMM's fixed per-launch inefficiency at small M, and the batched GEMM's regime — predict the crossover, and does a latency-floor roofline per group predict it prospectively?**

### B. Motivating evidence
- Session 4 PA (established): C3 − H = +1.512 (1 s), +0.846 (4 s), +0.455 (6 s), −0.331 ms (8 s), all with intervals; the Phase-3 affine predictor (E9) fails by ≈ 0.5 ms at 1/4/6 s with the wrong sign at 6 s (`docs/phase4/e9_retrospective_session4.json`) — the difference is not affine in frames: the observed curve lies above the chord, i.e. concave.
- Optimized versions (standalone, unpaired, same session): HG 1.39 vs C3MG 2.25 ms at 100 ms; HG 28.73 vs C3MG 23.56 ms at 8 s — the flip survives freezing and max-autotune.
- Trace efficiency (session 4, node-level, `ideal_costs_*`): GEMM class per frame at 100 ms — C3 114, C3G 148, C3MG 118, HG 76 µs/frame; at 8 s — C3 20.6, C3G 20.8, C3MG 18.2, HG 21.4 µs/frame. Per-frame GEMM cost falls 5.5× (C3), 7.1× (C3G), 6.5× (C3MG) and 3.6× (HG) from 11 to 801 frames (fixed per-launch inefficiency amortized), and only at 8 s does the padded path's GEMM class exceed the per-band path's (+0.47 ms over C3G).
- Pilot C0 (`docs/phase4/stage2/results/probe_c0_band_layout.json`, analysis only): the pinned layout has 60 bands with widths 6–130 bins (sum 1,979; the 3,958 `freq_indices` come from the stereo doubling of the indices, and the complex factor is applied separately, so each projection's input width is 4 × band width), so pad-and-batch multiplies the band-projection work by **3.94** (74.6 % of the padded work is padding), a fraction independent of frames. Established for this checkpoint.
- Observed shape (established): the C3 − H curve lies above the affine chord through its end points. Conjecture (not established, and not implied by the simple model below): the crossover comes from the per-band path's launch-floor inefficiency at small M (60 tiny GEMMs per projection) versus the batched path's 3.94× FLOP waste once the batched GEMM is compute-bound.

### C. Mechanism and competing explanations
1. **Latency-floor vs waste (candidate model):** T_band(F) ≈ Σ_bands max(t_floor, FLOPs_i(F)/peak) and T_batched(F) ≈ max(t_floor, 3.94·FLOPs(F)/peak). This naive model does **not** by itself reproduce the observed shape: both terms are convex in F, and once the batched term is linear each per-band GEMM leaving its floor *increases* the slope of the difference, whereas the observed differences lie above the affine chord. The model's parameters (floors, per-band efficiencies, the batched GEMM's efficiency at small M) are unmeasured, so its crossover position and direction are not derivable; with equal widths (waste 1.0) it permits a compute-bound tie rather than a batched win. What it does predict qualitatively: less dispersion makes batching cheaper and delays the loss of its advantage; higher per-launch floors favour batching at small M.
2. **Alternative — attention/body effects:** H changes only the band-split projections by construction; the traces establish equal SDPA totals (3.65–3.69 ms at 8 s) and nothing further about the rest of the body, so the body's equality is an arm-definition assumption, not a trace result.
3. **Alternative — kernel selection at large M:** the batched GEMM at 8 s may fall to a worse config (autotune/heuristics); the HG vs C3G GEMM-class delta of +0.47 ms at 8 s is consistent with either 3.94× waste on a small sub-part or selection. A per-kernel comparison of the band-projection GEMMs can separate them only between arms compiled with the same GEMM backend and options (C3G vs HG: both reduce-overhead + freezing, cuBLAS/CUTLASS), not between C3MG (max-autotune, Triton templates) and HG.
What distinguishes now: nothing — the per-frame efficiency curves show amortization on both sides but do not select a mechanism.

### D. Intended generality
Ragged-K grouped computation: band-split audio models (BS-RoFormer, Mel-Band RoFormer variants, multi-band front-ends), multi-resolution heads, per-group projections with unequal input widths — distinct from the ragged-M grouped-GEMM setting of MoE (MegaBlocks) that the literature covers. Invariants (conjectured): crossover position is a function of the waste factor, the per-launch floors and the batched GEMM's efficiency curve; with equal widths (waste 1.0) the two strategies do the same work and the model permits a tie at large M with batching ahead at small M; a grouped ragged-K GEMM on the target would change the question (torch 2.8 gates `_grouped_mm` to compute capability 9.0; sm120 has none). A second checkpoint with the same 60-band layout is not new architecture; a different layout (BS-RoFormer's 62 bands, different dispersion) is the informative change.

### E. Closest prior work
Abdelfattah et al., batched/variable-size GEMM (ISC 2016; note 14); MegaBlocks and TMA-adaptive grouped GEMM (padding cost 1.7–20.4 % on H800; F3); cuBLAS grouped GEMM (1.2× over looping); llama.cpp PR #27637 kernel choice by batch size (note 15); TensorRT optimization profiles (note 16); Chen's R_floor (note 9) and the launch-floor literature (F5). Prior work establishes ragged-M batching costs and floors; **this study establishes** a length-dependent flip between two ragged-K strategies on one GPU with intervals, a failed affine predictor, and the per-frame efficiency curves. **Remains:** a mechanism-level predictor validated prospectively on a new layout, and whether an sm120 ragged-K grouped kernel would dominate both.

### F. Potential contribution
A decision rule for deployment (strategy by chunk length and band layout) and a validated per-group latency-floor model; a boundary for when building grouped ragged-K kernels is worth it. Users: audio-separation deployers, band-split model authors, kernel authors choosing what to build. Domain-specific, but the modelling question (floors vs waste in ragged groups) is broader.

### G. Smallest discriminating experiment
Matched C3G vs HG (same compile options and GEMM backend) at 100 ms, 1 s, 4 s, 6 s, 8 s (registered protocol) with node traces, and separately C3MG vs HG for the deployment ranking. Discrimination at the kernel level is limited to C3G vs HG: under waste (1), the band-projection GEMMs of HG carry ≈ 3.9× the FLOPs of C3G's at every length with the same kernel family; under selection (3), HG's projection kernels change config between 6 s and 8 s. A synthetic equal-width layout (waste 1.0) as a structural variant (the Phase-3 E5 variant machinery exists) predicts, under the model, batching ahead at small M and a tie at large M — no reversal.

### H. Transfer or boundary test
BS-RoFormer's layout (different dispersion) — new data; a GPU with a different per-launch floor (H100-class) shifts the crossover in a predicted direction; the equal-width variant is the boundary case.

### I. Decision criteria
Supports: a parameterized floor/efficiency model, fitted on measured per-band and batched GEMM curves and registered prospectively, predicts the C3G/HG crossover within the declared threshold on a held-out length, and the equal-width variant removes the reversal. Weakens: crossover explained by kernel selection (3). Rejects: no crossover after optimization, or the model mispredicts by more than the A/A spread. Labels as in Candidate 1; the E9 failure is not refitted — a new prospective registration is required.

### J. Effort
Moderate: variants exist (E5 machinery), arms exist; one pod session of ≈ 2 h for the five lengths with traces; the equal-width variant ≈ 1 day; the BS-RoFormer transfer needs a second checkpoint and layout (≈ 3 days, quality gates included). Grouped ragged-K kernel development is a separate program, not part of this test.

## Rejected or deferred alternatives (with the reason and its basis)
- **Megakernel / persistent-kernel execution** (Cohere 2026-09-08, MPK arXiv 2512.22219, Hazy "No Bubbles"): deferred by the owner's scope; the study's launch floor is already low after graph repair (F5/F6), so the expected mechanism (bubble removal) has little room here — a valid deferral on measured evidence, not a rejection of the technique.
- **FP8 / quantization** (F8): needs model-quality work outside the scope; not rejected on performance grounds.
- **Attention backend choice** (F4): static priority, small deltas measured in Phase 3; low expected value.
- **Refitting E9 on the new lengths:** forbidden by the instructions and scientifically void (the held-out points were seen).
- **Driver/GPU variation** (580.159.03 vs 580.178.04; three cards): confounded by design; no experiment proposed.
- **sm120 autotune config space** (incidental #5: `triton_mm` configs rejected for exceeding the 101,376-byte shared-memory limit): an engineering improvement of the candidate set, plausible but not a research question; recorded for the max-autotune users.
- **Inlining-off cost** (−0.2 / −0.08 ms; invisible in device time): unresolved but small; not pursued.
- Candidate 2 is not rejected on evidence; it is narrowed because the upstream fix and documentation already explain it (see its I).

## Limitations
Pilot probes ran on a shared engineering host with coarse event timing and idle-clock behaviour between iterations; they are directional. The Stage-1 evidence is one checkpoint, one model family, RTX 5090s only; the trace attribution is descriptive (node-level, caches disabled). The prior-work search used web search and the Phase-3 notes; access to some venues' full texts was not attempted, and an unsuccessful search is not proof of absence.

## Recommendation
Candidate 1 is recommended for deeper investigation: it is mechanistic, cheap to test, already supported by matched evidence on two GPUs and by an isolated pilot that separates warming from generic effects, and it generalizes beyond this model to any producer→consumer operand pattern in latency-bound inference (casts, dequantization, conversions). Candidate 3 is the better domain result but costs a pod session per step and a second checkpoint; Candidate 2 is a rule.

**The most promising next research question is whether the loss of producer-side operand warming (L2 or translation residency) predictably offsets the gain from removing redundant cast kernels in latency-bound inference, because the corrected evidence shows unchanged GEMM kernels slowing by 44–47 % of the removed cast time at the 100 ms point on two GPUs and the isolated pilot reproduces it with an operand-specific warming contrast. The smallest experiment that would materially change this assessment is the matched C3G vs C3G-plus-touch pair at 100 ms under the registered protocol, where the touch reads the frozen fp16 operands of the 60 dominant projections (identity verified) and an unrelated-buffer touch is the control, judged on the GEMM-class device-time recovery of that subset: no recovery with verified operands would show the pilot does not transfer to graph replay, and equal recovery from the unrelated touch would reduce the effect to a generic preceding-kernel artefact.**
